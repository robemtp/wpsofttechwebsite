The Problem

Most shops run six systems that don't talk

The quote lives in a spreadsheet. Purchase orders live in email. Inventory lives in one app and the shop floor lives on a clipboard. Training records live in a binder, and at the end of the month somebody re-keys all of it into the accounting package by hand.

Every one of those hand-offs is a place where a number changes, a charge gets missed, or a certification quietly expires. WP ERP puts all of it on one record, so the quote, the PO, the receipt, the work order, the storage, the shipment and the invoice are the same chain of events — not six copies of it.

Enter it once

What the salesperson quotes is what purchasing buys, what the floor builds, and what accounting bills. No re-keying between systems, so there is nothing to reconcile.

History is never overwritten

Records are append-only. A correction posts as a new entry that reverses the old one, and both stay visible — so an auditor can follow what actually happened.

Price and cost changes don't rewrite the past

A rate or cost revision applies going forward. Last quarter's invoices and last quarter's job costs never move underneath you.

Nobody approves their own work

Segregation of duties is enforced by the software, not by policy. The person who creates and submits cannot be the person who approves.

The Suite

Every module, one record

Turn on what you need. Every module writes to the same database and the same ledger, so a quote becomes a purchase order becomes a work order becomes an invoice without anyone typing it twice. New modules land in the same ledger as the ones already running — turning one on later is a settings change, not a second system.

  • Buy it and stock it

    Getting materials in, and knowing what you are holding.

    • Purchasing

      Requisition, approval, purchase order, receipt, vendor bill — in that order, each one matched to the last. Partial receipts are handled, and three-way match between the PO, the receipt and the vendor invoice means you don’t pay for goods nobody took in. Vendors carry their own price lists and lead times.

    • Supply chain

      Demand against supply, in one place: what’s on hand, what’s committed to jobs, what’s on order and what’s short. Reorder points surface the shortage while there is still time to do something about it, and vendor performance is measured on what actually arrived and when — not on what was promised.

    • Inventory

      Parts and SKUs with on-hand quantities by location, tracked by lot or by serial number where it matters, and committed stock kept separate from available so a job never gets promised parts that are already spoken for. Cycle counts instead of a once-a-year shutdown, and costing your way — average, FIFO or standard — posting straight to the ledger.

  • Build it and move it

    The floor, the machines, the yard, and keeping the people on it safe.

    • Manufacturing

      Work orders and routings released to the floor — a full manufacturing execution system (MES), not a work-order list. Operators clock on and off at a station, materials are issued and scrap is recorded as it happens, and in-process quality checks are part of the step rather than a form filled in afterwards.

      Labour and material booked on the floor flow straight into job cost — so you can see what a job is costing while it is still running, not a month after it shipped. The MES runs on the same record as the rest of the suite, not alongside it.

    • Maintenance

      Your own plant and equipment on the same record as the work it does — assets, meters, service history, and preventive schedules that raise a work order on hours run or days elapsed instead of when somebody remembers.

      Parts come off the same inventory production draws from and labour books the same way, so what it costs to keep a machine running sits next to what the machine produced. When a machine goes down it takes its jobs with it, and the schedule shows that before the promise date does.

    • Warehousing & custody

      Warehouse management down to zone and bay across every site, with every touch an item takes — received, placed, moved, re-crated, inspected, staged, released — timestamped, attributed to a person, and priced at the rate in force that day.

      Storage bills once per item per month however many times it moves. Handling bills per touch. Built for 3PL custody work, where the item belongs to your customer and the record has to hold up.

Module in detail — Warehousing: warehouse management & 3PL custody

Handling is the revenue most systems quietly lose

This is the module WP ERP grew out of, built with a rigging and crating company that was capturing handling hours in one system and re-keying them into another to invoice. It is worth a close look, because the rest of the suite is built the same way.

A heavy item might be received by crane, moved three times, re-crated, shrink-wrapped, inspected, staged and loaded before it ever leaves. Most warehouse systems bill the storage and forget the touches — or bill the storage twice because the item changed bays. WP ERP treats custody as the source of truth: storage bills once per item per month no matter how many times it moves, and handling bills per touch at the rate in force the day it happened.

Moving an item doesn't re-bill rent

Occupancy follows the item across bays and yard overflow as one continuous storage cycle.

Rate changes don't rewrite history

A tariff revision applies going forward. A customer on a negotiated card keeps their rates until renewal, and old invoices never move.

Partial releases stay honest

Release two of three and the siblings keep accruing storage on their own clock.

Nobody approves their own invoice

The system refuses self-approval. The person who creates and submits cannot be the person who approves.

Inside the Platform

What every module sits on

The modules are what you click. This is what they run on — a real double-entry accounting core rather than a set of apps with a reporting layer glued on top, plus the controls that decide who gets to click what.

  • Ledger & posting engine

    The substrate every module posts through. Posting rules carry activate and retire versions, so the way a transaction posted last year is still on the record when this year’s rules differ — and nothing reaches the ledger except through it.

  • Pricing, rate sheets & tariffs

    A standard price list plus per-customer negotiated cards. Revisions are dated, so a customer can hold old rates until renewal and finished documents never change.

  • Trial balance & drill-down

    Trial balance with verification, rollups by dimension, and drill from any figure back to the posting request that produced it.

  • Labels & scanning

    Batch-printed Code 128 barcodes, phone or handheld scanning, and floor views built for someone wearing gloves.

  • Users, roles & permissions

    Add a user, give them a role, and the menus, screens and approvals they see follow from it. Underneath: a full audit log of every change, segregation of duties the software enforces rather than a policy that asks nicely, and tenant separation built into the data layer instead of the screens.

  • REST API & webhooks

    Documented endpoints with an OpenAPI spec. Every screen is an API call, so integration isn’t an afterthought.
